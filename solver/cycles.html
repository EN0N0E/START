The False Doublet Evidence critical

Repeated strikes at the same location — dismissed as "pure coincidence"

Academic literature identifies these as "false doublets" — pairs of craters at the same spot formed millions of years apart. The lattice model reframes them as proof of node reactivation.

  • 1,812 MyrSudbury (1,849 Ma) + Wanapitei (37 Ma) — nested, same location
  • 625 MyrSuvasvesi South (710 Ma) + North (85 Ma) — Finland, same spot
  • 180 MyrClearwater East (460 Ma) + West (290 Ma) — Quebec, Node ε
  • 0.8 MyrRies (14.8 Ma) + Steinheim (14.0 Ma) — Bavaria, Node κ region

The 2015 Ar-Ar dating paper on Clearwater concludes the proximity is "most likely pure coincidence." Four independent re-dating studies on four separate sites all reach the same conclusion. The lattice model offers a single geometric explanation for all of them.

These nodes have the same lattice-line density as active nodes but no confirmed impacts within 15°. The model predicts undiscovered structures at each.

Conclusion: The lattice cycle has operated for at least 2.2 billion years (62 confirmed cycles). Over Earth's full 4.5 Ga lifetime, approximately 125 cycles have occurred.

We detect ~27% of all activations in the raw count — but correcting for the 70% of Earth's surface that is ocean (where crust is recycled every ~200 Myr by subduction), the adjusted detection rate on land reaches 91%. Nearly every activation that could be found, has been.

The literature's own estimate of ~36,000 craters >30 km (lunar scaling) vs. ~200 confirmed means we see <1% of all impacts. But impacts clustering at lattice nodes — not uniformly distributed — is what the eigenmode hypothesis predicts.

With ~350 craters still undiscovered on exposed land (Hergarten & Kenkmann 2015) and 14 million km² of unsurveyed Antarctic ice, the 8 dormant nodes are testable predictions.
