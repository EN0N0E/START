62 Myr Biodiversity Cycle

Rohde & Muller (2005, Nature) analyzed 36,380 marine genera across 542 Myr and found a 62 ±3 Myr cycle at 99% confidence. Independently confirmed by Melott (2008) using the Paleobiology Database at p = 0.001, with only 1.6 Myr phase displacement between datasets. The Big Five extinctions occur during declining phases. Roughly 2× the ~27-30 Myr impact/extinction period — a full galactic vertical oscillation produces plane crossings every ~31 Myr.

2. PERIODICITY MECHANISMS

Galactic Plane Oscillation (30-35 Myr half-period)

The solar system oscillates vertically through the galactic disk as it orbits the center. Full oscillation period ~62-66 Myr produces galactic mid-plane crossings every ~31-33 Myr. During crossings, enhanced gravitational perturbation of the Oort cloud sends comet showers into the inner solar system.

Rampino & Stothers (1984) and Schwartz & James (1984) proposed this simultaneously with the Nemesis hypothesis. The ~33 Myr half-period is broadly consistent with the ~27-30 Myr extinction/impact periodicity, though exact phase matching remains debated.

Medvedev & Melott (2007, ApJ): Extragalactic cosmic rays concentrated on the galaxy's north side. When the solar system emerges above the disk, it loses magnetic shielding and experiences heightened cosmic ray flux.

Nemesis Hypothesis (Davis, Hut & Muller 1984)

A dim companion star ("Nemesis") at 25,000-150,000 AU with ~26 Myr orbital period periodically passes through the Oort cloud, sending comet showers Earthward.

Effectively ruled out: Orbital decay from galactic tides would increase its period by ~20% over 250 Myr, incompatible with the observed regularity of the extinction cycle (Whitmire 2016). The WISE all-sky survey found no companion star fitting the description.

Planet X Hypothesis (Whitmire & Matese 1985, 2016)

An undiscovered planet beyond Neptune (1-15 Earth masses, ~100-210 AU) with a tilted, precessing orbit periodically passes through the Kuiper belt every ~28 Myr, scattering comets inward.

Whitmire (2016, MNRAS Letters) reconsidered the model and argued it better fits the extinction periodicity than Nemesis because a Planet X orbit maintains stable periodicity over 500 Myr. The independently-motivated Batygin & Brown (2016) Planet Nine has some overlapping parameter ranges but different orbital characteristics (~700 AU, ~15,000 yr period — too short for 27 Myr comet showers).

The WISE survey ruled out Saturn-sized objects to 28,000 AU and Jupiter-sized to 82,000 AU, eliminating the Tyche variant (Matese & Whitmire's ~4 Jupiter mass body at ~15,000 AU). A super-Earth at ~100-200 AU remains below WISE detection limits.

Alvarez & Muller (1984) — Crater Age Periodicity

Walter Alvarez and Richard Muller analyzed 11-13 impact crater ages and found a 28.4 Myr periodicity, matching the Raup-Sepkoski extinction cycle in both period and phase.

Rampino & Caldeira (2015, MNRAS) updated this with 37 precisely-dated craters and found 25.8 ±0.6 Myr in crater ages and 27.0 ±0.7 Myr in extinctions — remarkably similar. However, Lyytinen et al. (2009) showed reliable detection of periodicity from existing crater data is "impossible unless all impacts have been due to periodic comet showers."

The crater record suffers from: small sample sizes (~200 confirmed structures, only ~26 with robust ages), dating uncertainties, and the complication that most impacts are from aperiodic asteroids.

3. ANTIPODAL FOCUSING

Mechanism

A large impact generates seismic waves that propagate through Earth's interior. Due to the planet's spherical geometry, both body waves (P, S) and surface waves (Rayleigh) reconverge at the antipodal point — the exact opposite side of the globe. This "seismic lens" effect concentrates energy along the axis of symmetry between impact and antipode.

Key Quantitative Results

Meschede, Myhrvold & Tromp (2011, GJI) used SPECFEM3D_GLOBE with realistic 3D Earth models:

  • Peak antipodal displacements: ~4 meters (reduced from 15 m predicted by symmetric models)
  • Peak stress values exceeding 50 bar, strain exceeding 10⁻⁵
  • Stresses exceed typical triggering thresholds (1-10 bar) by an order of magnitude
  • Wave interference creates "channels" of peak stress 5× greater than surrounding areas — "mantle chimneys"

Key Proponents

Hagstrum (2005) — Antipodal Hotspot Pairs

Of 45 "primary" hotspots, 22 (49%) form antipodal pairs within observed hotspot drift limits. Monte Carlo simulations: p < 0.01 (non-random at >99% confidence). Ages of both hotspots in each pair are similar (≤10 Myr difference). All pairs include at least one oceanic hotspot opposite continental volcanism.

Hagstrum, J.T. (2005). "Antipodal hotspots and bipolar catastrophes." Earth and Planetary Science Letters.

Richards et al. (2015) — Chicxulub Triggered Deccan

Proposed that the Chicxulub impact generated seismic shaking equivalent to Mw 9+ globally, mobilizing existing magma within the plume head beneath India. ~70% of total Deccan lavas (Wai subgroup) erupted within ~100,000 years after impact.

Important: Richards explicitly noted Chicxulub is ~5,000 km from Deccan's antipode and rejected the strict antipodal focusing mechanism. Instead proposed global seismic shaking + magma mobilization (not antipodal energy concentration). Seismic energy densities of 0.1-1.0 J/m³ in upper ~200 km of mantle.

Richards, M.A. et al. (2015). "Triggering of the largest Deccan eruptions by the Chicxulub impact." GSA Bulletin.

Criticisms & Limitations
  • Energy deficit: Direct melting at the antipode requires energy orders of magnitude higher than seismic dissipation can deliver (Meschede et al. 2011). Mechanism is disruption/mobilization, not melting.
  • Chicxulub-Deccan mismatch: ~5,000 km off antipodal — the best-studied case doesn't strictly fit.
  • No impact markers at LIP sites: No tektite strewn fields or impact signatures found near continental LIPs.
  • Melosh's critique: No firm evidence impacts can induce volcanism even near the crater, let alone at distance.
  • Deccan predates Chicxulub: Several Myr of pre-impact eruptions, though at lower volume.
  • Missing ocean craters: Theory predicts undiscovered structures in the Indian and Pacific Oceans.

4. K-Pg EXTINCTION (66 Ma)

76% species loss. Chicxulub impact (180 km crater, Yucatán) confirmed by global iridium anomaly (Alvarez et al. 1980). Impactor: ~10-15 km diameter asteroid, ~60° impact angle, 20 km/s. The impactor struck sulfate evaporites and carbonates — uniquely deadly target rock.

Deccan Traps — Concurrent Volcanism

The Deccan Traps (western India) erupted ~1.3 million km³ of basalt between ~68-63 Ma. ~70% of the volume (Wai subgroup) erupted within ~100,000 years after Chicxulub. Richards et al. (2015) proposed the impact mobilized pre-existing magma.

The Deccan-Chicxulub debate ("volcanism vs. impact") has largely converged on both contributing, with the impact as the primary extinction trigger and Deccan providing background stress + post-impact warming.

5. END-PERMIAN EXTINCTION (252 Ma)

96% species loss — the "Great Dying." Most severe extinction in Earth history. Killed 81% of marine species, 70% of terrestrial vertebrates, the only known mass extinction of insects.

Primary Driver: Siberian Traps

Largest known continental flood basalt province: ~3-4 million km³ of lava, ~7 million km² area (comparable to Australia). Erupted between ~252.24 and ~251.35 Ma (~0.9 Myr total). U-Pb zircon dating places the main eruptive pulse within 300 kyr of the extinction boundary.

Kill Mechanisms

  • CO₂ + Methane: Up to 170,000 Gt CO₂ released, plus methane from contact metamorphism of Tunguska Basin coal/petroleum deposits. Global warming of 8-10°C.
  • Ocean anoxia/euxinia: >80% of seafloor under anoxic conditions. Uranium isotope evidence shows global oxygen depletion.
  • Ocean acidification: Carbonate dissolution documented in boundary sections.
  • Hydrogen sulfide: Euxinic oceans produced toxic H₂S gas, destroying the ozone layer via photolysis.
  • Acid rain: SO₂ from eruptions.
Wilkes Land Connection (Proposed)

The proposed Wilkes Land crater (~480 km, under Antarctic ice) dates to ~250 Ma and lies near the antipode of the Siberian Traps. If confirmed, this would be the strongest antipodal impact-LIP pair. The Wilkes Land structure is detected by gravity anomaly but remains undrilled and age-uncertain.

In the bhole projection lattice, Wilkes Land is 8.3° from Fornax A — one of the tightest impact-bhole proximities.

6. END-ORDOVICIAN EXTINCTION (444 Ma)

85% species loss. Two distinct pulses driven by Hirnantian glaciation — the only mass extinction primarily attributed to cooling rather than warming.

Two Pulses

PulseTimingDriverKilled
Pulse 1Early HirnantianGlacial onset, ~100m sea-level fall, ~5°C coolingShallow shelf fauna (habitat loss)
Pulse 2Late HirnantianDeglaciation, transgression, anoxic/euxinic waters flood shelvesCold-adapted Hirnantian fauna

Key Data

  • Glaciation: Ice sheets on Gondwana (N. Africa, Arabia, S. America). 50-250 million km³ ice volume (≥LGM). CO₂ fell from ~7,000 to ~4,400 ppm.
  • Sea level: ~100 m regression. At least 4 distinct lowstands within the Hirnantian (Calner et al. 2021).
  • Ocean anoxia: HOAE onset <20 kyr before peak glaciation. ~15.8% of seafloor euxinic (vs <1% today). Persisted >3 Myr after the second pulse.
  • δ¹⁸O shift: +2 to 4‰ (combined ice volume + cooling signal).
  • HICE: Positive δ¹³C shift of +3 to +7‰ — carbon burial + weathering feedback.
Volcanic Trigger: Alborz LIP

The Alborz LIP (Iran), with U-Pb zircon ages 468.7 ±0.3 to ~442.5 Ma, spans the entire Late Ordovician including the Hirnantian. Mercury anomalies (Hg/TOC) documented in South China, Poland, and Laurentia support volcanic forcing. Two Hg/TOC spikes correlate with the two extinction pulses.

A 2025 Nature Communications study proposed volcanism + basalt weathering drove the 25-Myr interval of Ordovician cooling (470-445 Ma) culminating in the Hirnantian.

GRB Hypothesis (Melott & Thomas 2004)

A nearby gamma-ray burst (<6,000 ly) could have destroyed ~50% of the ozone layer, triggering UV stress + glaciation. Selectivity evidence: shallow-water organisms hit hardest (consistent with UV kill). Piran & Jimenez (2014): 50% chance a lethal GRB hit Earth in the past 500 Myr.

Status: Plausible but untestable. No unique geological fingerprint. Largely eclipsed by volcanism-driven models with testable predictions. Not mentioned in Harper (2023) major review.

7. LATE DEVONIAN EXTINCTION (372 Ma)

75% species loss. Not a single event but a prolonged biodiversity crisis across the Frasnian-Famennian (F-F) boundary. Two discrete black-shale horizons (Lower and Upper Kellwasser), with the Upper being the main extinction pulse.

Algeo-Scheckler Hypothesis: Plants Kill the Oceans

The evolution of deep-rooting land plants (Archaeopteris forests, 30 m tall) enhanced continental silicate weathering by ~10×. Deep roots fracture bedrock, stabilize thick soils, amplify chemical weathering via organic acids and mycorrhizal fungi. This flushed phosphorus into the oceans, triggering eutrophication, algal blooms, and widespread marine anoxia — the Kellwasser events.

  • CO₂ drawdown: Atmospheric CO₂ fell from ~2,000-4,000 ppm (Early Devonian) to <1,000 ppm (Late Devonian) via Urey reaction + organic carbon burial
  • Cooling: ~5-7°C tropical cooling at F-F boundary (conodont δ¹⁸O). Famennian diamictites confirm glaciation by latest Devonian.
  • ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr rise: From ~0.7078 to ~0.7085 through Middle-Late Devonian, consistent with enhanced continental weathering.
Viluy Traps & Volcanism

Viluy Traps (eastern Siberia): >1 million km³ of magma across >320,000 km². Two ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar phases: ~376.7 ±3.4 Ma and ~364.4 ±3.4 Ma. First phase overlaps Kellwasser extinction within error.

Mercury anomalies (Bond & Grasby 2018, Geology): Hg/TOC spikes at Upper Kellwasser across Germany, France, Morocco, North America. Coronene co-enrichment supports volcanic source.

Modern consensus: multifactorial — plant weathering provided background forcing, volcanic episodes (Viluy, Kola, Pripyat-Dnieper-Donets LIPs, Cerberean Caldera) pushed past tipping points.

8. END-TRIASSIC EXTINCTION (201 Ma)

80% species loss. Coincides with the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province (CAMP) — one of the largest LIPs, emplaced as Pangaea began to rift open.

CAMP

  • Area: ~11 million km² (fragments now across 4 continents)
  • Volume: ~2-3 million km³ of basalt
  • Duration: main phase ~600 kyr, but may have erupted in 4 discrete pulses
  • CO₂ release triggered 3-6°C warming, ocean acidification, anoxia

No confirmed impact: Manicouagan crater (100 km, Quebec) was initially proposed but dated to 214 Ma — 14 Myr too early. No other candidate crater matches the 201 Ma boundary.

CAMP-Impact Gap

The 14 Myr gap between Manicouagan (214 Ma) and the Triassic-Jurassic boundary (201 Ma) is one of the strongest arguments against antipodal impact-LIP triggering for this extinction. CAMP volcanism began independently of any known impact.

9. YOUNGER DRYAS IMPACT HYPOTHESIS

Proposed by Firestone et al. (2007, PNAS): fragments of a large comet struck or exploded over North America ~12,800 Cal BP, triggering the ~1,200-year Younger Dryas cold reversal, megafaunal extinction, and Clovis culture disappearance.

Evidence Summary

EvidenceStrengthStatus
Abu Hureyra melt glassStrongTemps >2,200°C rule out all terrestrial processes except impact/airburst. Melted quartz (1720°C), chromite (2265°C), magnetite boiling (~2623°C).
MicrospherulesStrongReplicated at 13+ sites after methodological corrections. LeCompte (2012) identified errors in Surovell's failed replication.

NanodiamondsModerateReplicated at 8 sites. But Daulton et al. (2017): "no lonsdaleite" — graphene/graphane-oxide misidentified.
Biomass burningModerateWolbach: 152-site dataset. But Marlon (2009): 35-site analysis shows no continent-wide fire spike.
IridiumWeakInconsistent across sites. Paquay: authigenesis, not impact.
Black matsWeakPigati (2012): form through normal wetland processes at all ages.

Shocked quartzAbsentCritical gap. Only irregular fractures (lower pressure), not diagnostic PDFs.
Impact craterAbsentNo crater found. Hiawatha dated to 58 Ma — ruled out.
Key Criticisms

Holliday et al. (2023, Earth-Science Reviews): 17 co-authors, most thorough critique. Concluded evidence "involves flawed methodologies, inappropriate assumptions, questionable conclusions, misstatements of fact, misleading information, unsupported claims, irreproducible observations, logical fallacies."

Green et al. (2025, PLOS ONE): GISP2 Pt spike occurred ~45-50 years AFTER YD onset — attributed to Icelandic fissure eruption.

Reproducibility failures: Surovell (2009) could not replicate magnetic mineral spike. Blind test at Lubbock Lake (2016): two labs got different results from identical samples.

Chronological synchroneity fails: Meltzer et al. (2014): markers don't form temporally synchronous layer. Jorgeson et al. (2020): radiocarbon simulation shows synchronous deposition "extremely unlikely."

Mainstream explanation: Meltwater disruption of Atlantic thermohaline circulation (Broecker 1989). Similar cold reversals (Older Dryas, 8.2 kyr event) occurred without any impact.

Data integrity: Allen West convicted of fraud for falsely claiming geologist credentials. Tell el-Hammam paper retracted April 2025 for image manipulation.
